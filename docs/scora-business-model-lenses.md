# Scora · Strategy Working Paper #1 — Business Model, Four Lenses

> Rev. 2, July 2026. The same app (multi-source athletic data, visualized beautifully, interpreted honestly, coached for money) argued four ways: to Peter Thiel, to nobody but ourselves, from inside Strava's product org, and — the premise underneath it all — built to be acquired by Strava. This is a strategic pressure-test exercise to find direction and weaknesses, **not a set of new directives.** (Text version of the HTML working paper.)
>
> Market context: AthleteData $39/mo ($299/yr, raised from $25); Baseline $0 / $12/mo / $249 lifetime; Strava Premium ~$80/yr. Acquisition comp: Runna (Apr 2025, ~$40M ARR run rate, est. $100M+ valuation, £8M raised).

## Snapshot of the field

- **AthleteData:** chat coach, 500+ paid in 6 weeks, no native app.
- **Baseline:** dashboard archive, no interpretation, no plans yet.
- **Strava:** the network; API closed to third-party AI; a proven buyer (Runna, est. $100M+).
- **Nobody** is native, and nobody bridges meaning to action.

---

## Lens I · The Thiel Pitch

*"Competition is for losers. What do you know that nobody else believes?"*

- Undercutting an incumbent ("they charge $39, we'll charge $19") concedes a competitive market, which converges to zero profit. Price from value, not below the incumbent.
- **The secret / contrarian truth:** coaching advice is commoditizing to zero — every frontier LLM can already give it. What does NOT commoditize is the **longitudinal, interpreted model of one specific athlete's physiology**, compounding daily. Devices own sensors; platforms own activities; **nobody owns the meaning layer.** That's the unclaimed monopoly. *Charge for memory, not for sentences.*
- **Start embarrassingly small:** self-coached ultra/trail athletes as the beachhead, then concentric expansion; the real TAM is interpretation of personal health data (the consumer front-end to healthspan).
- **Moat audit:** proprietary tech is weak today (weekend-clone velocity) → manufacture IP via a named, published, defended score. Switching costs are the honest moat (every day of interpreted history raises the exit cost). Brand/taste is a rare genuine asymmetry.
- **Free tier = distribution land-grab** (not charity): acquires the niche, builds the dataset, manufactures switching costs. But the paid line sits on the monopoly asset: **free is today, paid is your history.** Price confident ($39+).
- His closing question: *"Garmin or WHOOP revokes your API access. What's left?"* Answer direction: become the layer athletes demand their devices connect to; get to proprietary scoring fast.

## Lens II · Bold Without Permission

*No fund to return. Optimize for sovereignty, profit, and taste.*

- Matches the actual life (staff role, nights-and-weekends). At $99/yr flat, 1,000 subscribers is a real business; 5,000 is life-changing. Demand is proven (Julian's 500+ in six weeks).
- Moves that would horrify a board, and might be right: **one price, no tiers** ($99/yr, Basecamp move); or **paid-only from day one** (a paywall can't hide a product nobody would pay for); a deliberate **lifetime "Founding Athletes" tier** (elegant bootstrapping); **privacy as principle**; **iOS-only, native-only, opinionated.**
- Freemium here is optional — only as the marketing budget you don't have. This lens is entitled to skip freemium entirely; it needs 1,000 people who love it, not scale.
- **The cost, stated plainly:** velocity. A nights-and-weekends cadence may be structurally too slow for this market. Works only if scope is kept brutally small, or if the goal is craft and optionality rather than racing.

## Lens III · If We Were Strava

*The only fitness freemium that ever truly worked, read as teacher and threat.*

- **Why Strava's freemium worked:** free users created the product (segments, routes, the social graph). Every free athlete made paid more valuable and the network harder to leave. **Freemium works when the free tier is an appreciating asset; it fails when it's charity with a funnel.**
- **Scora's version of the segment = the cohort.** Anonymized, aggregated physiological benchmarks ("your HRV suppression after threshold vs athletes your age at your volume"). Confidence scales with the free base. Design the schema day one; the free tier becomes **the factory that manufactures the paid tier's authority.**
- **Strava's scars as instructions:** never take away, only add above (the 2020 backlash → additive-forever rule); the API lockdown is both a warning (platforms amputate) and a market signal (interpretation is valuable enough to fence off).
- **The predator reading — why the incumbent hasn't won:** Strava **cannot do multi-source recovery fusion.** WHOOP/Oura/Garmin are its rivals and won't feed its moat; its social DNA points to sharing, not clinical interpretation. Same bind constrains Garmin and Apple. **Scora's defensible position is Switzerland:** neutral ground between armed platforms, loyal to the athlete.

## Lens IV · The Strava Exit (the premise all along)

*Build the organ the buyer is already collecting.*

- With acquisition as the goal, the other three lenses become instruments: Thiel supplies the corp-dev story, the bootstrap lens supplies the discipline, Lens III flips Strava from teacher to **customer.**
- **The comp:** Runna, April 2025 — three years launch to exit, ~$40M ARR, est. $100M+, £119.99/yr, only £8M raised (life-changing founder math). Strava's rationale: users named structured guidance as the gap; building in-house cost years before an IPO.
- **Strava collects organs; one is missing.** Recover Athletics (injury, 2022), FatMap (maps, 2023), Runna (run training, 2025), The Breakaway (cycling, 2025) — the coaching stack, one organ at a time. Conspicuously absent: **the physiological readiness layer.** Strava has no HRV/sleep/recovery, and the owners (WHOOP, Oura, Garmin) are rivals. Their **API ban on third-party AI forces buy-over-build.**
- **What the product becomes:** the **readiness organ** — neutral multi-source fusion on official rails, a named score, native craft polished enough to drop into Strava Pro as the readiness tab. Secondary bidder Oura; tertiary Garmin (Firstbeat precedent); dark horse: an assistant platform wanting the canonical athlete-state endpoint (the Scora-as-MCP move).
- **Deltas this lens forces:** a small targeted raise stops being impure (an 18-36 month window; nights-and-weekends can't hit it); after paid validation, trade margin for slope; package the methodology for the data room; official API agreements are transferable assets (scrapers kill deals); manufacture quiet tension between bidders.
- **Discipline:** acquirers pay premiums for companies that don't need them; every buyer-shaped choice must pass the standalone test. Base rates: median exit $5-30M, many get no offer — Runna is the dream case, not the expectation.

## Where all four agree (convergence)

1. **Interpretation is the product; visualization is packaging.** Baseline is the proof by failure — beautiful charts, no verdict, reads as purposeless.
2. **The paid line is memory, not features.** Free is today; paid is your history/trends/season.
3. **A free tier must be an asset or not exist.** Distribution, brand, dataset, or diligence-grade growth — any justifies free. Charity justifies nothing.
4. **Undercutting is the weakest move.** AthleteData raising to $39 widens the umbrella — stand under it.
5. **Native craft is the wedge nobody else holds.** Twenty years of consumer design is the one asymmetry no competitor clones in a weekend — the exact thing Strava paid Runna for.

## The honest recommendation

**Operate Lens II. Build Lens III. Tell Lens I. Aim the whole thing at Lens IV.** Day-to-day = bootstrap discipline (brutal scope, iOS-native, one athlete type, one killer loop, cohort schema in the first commit). But: **pure bootstrapping and the acquisition window are incompatible** — a small targeted raise is the price of arriving while Strava is still shopping. The Thiel narrative is the memo you want Strava's corp dev writing about you.

### Concrete next moves
1. Validate paid-first, then trade margin for slope (private beta, 30-day trial, $8-12/mo or $99/yr).
2. Draw the free line at "today" (current-day viz + one interpreted readout, ephemeral; trends/history/cohort/planning behind the wall — forever additive).
3. Name the score, publish the score (moat + Firstbeat asset + data-room headline).
4. Write the Switzerland sentence into the brand ("loyal to the athlete, not the platform").
5. Open the small-raise conversation (sized so $30-50M is life-changing; 18-36mo window).
6. File the Garmin and Oura partner applications this quarter (data access now, buyer relationships later).
