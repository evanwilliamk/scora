# SCORA · Training Canon & Doctrine

> **Companion to the Master Context (Rev 5).** Status: v0.3. Purpose: the canon the app lives by. A deliberate blend of flow and perception philosophy with proven, hard training methodology, ingested from primary texts and synthesized into Scora's own doctrine. This file drives product decisions (the philosophy layer and the score's priors). Nothing enters without provenance. It is ADDITIVE canon enriching an otherwise-thin voice — not a new product direction.

---

## 0. Rules of this document

- **Provenance tiers.** [PRIMARY] = from the books via Evan's reading and notes, cited by book and chapter. [PRIMARY-AUDIO] = from podcast transcripts. [PUBLIC] = established, verifiable published methodology. [INFERENCE] = our extrapolation, always marked.
- **Expression firewall.** Principles restated in our own words only. No reproduced pace tables, plan schedules, or workout charts: those are the authors' published expression. Scora computes its own model from its own math; the canon informs the shape, never supplies the tables. Trademark care where relevant.
- **Ownership.** The Scora score remains Scora's proprietary methodology. Canon authors are cited influences in the methodology white paper alongside the scientific literature, never sources of the algorithm.
- **Conflict handling.** Where canon authors disagree with each other or with the forecaster thesis, the conflict gets a written adjudication in section 4. No silent harmonizing. A bible that argues back is the point.

## 1. The canon roster

### Wing A: State and perception (how the athlete reads and executes)

- **Richard Diaz** (My Best Race; Training the Dark Side; Flow: Breaking Barriers; RUN, Jan 2026). Flow as a trainable performance state; endurance as a body-breath-awareness dialogue; effort before pace; lactate tolerance ("the Dark Side"). Lab-grounded: four decades of VO₂, gait, and metabolic testing.
- **Matt Fitzgerald** (RUN: The Mind-Body Method of Running by Feel; How Bad Do You Want It?). The psychobiological model: perceived effort as the governing variable of endurance performance; running by feel as a trainable skill, not a vibe.
- **Steve Magness** (Do Hard Things). Toughness redefined as accurate appraisal of body signals and situation, not suppression of them. The scientific case that listening is performance, which is the third-witness thesis in book form.
- **Floris Gierman** (Extramilest; popularizer of Phil Maffetone's MAF low-heart-rate method). Aerobic base built under a hard HR cap, relaxed running, racing by feel and negative splits. Supporting weight, not a pillar. His structural core (the MAF cap and the monthly MAF test: pace at fixed heart rate over time) is a native receipts instrument, aerobic progress made falsifiable. Primary corpus is podcast/YouTube; underlying primary text is Maffetone's Big Book of Endurance Training and Racing.

### Wing B: Structure and load (what training should look like)

- **Jack Daniels** (Daniels' Running Formula). A single fitness value derived from race performance that prescribes exact training paces across defined intensities, plus a points system for accounting training stress. The direct intellectual ancestor of a falsifiable, numeric, pace-prescribing score.
- **Matt Fitzgerald** (80/20 Running, building on Seiler's intensity-distribution research). Roughly 80% of running below ventilatory threshold, 20% at moderate-to-high intensity; the documented failure mode is drifting into a 50/50 moderate mush that blunts adaptation.
- **Pete Pfitzinger & Scott Douglas** (Advanced Marathoning; Faster Road Racing). The block architecture: mesocycles, lactate-threshold emphasis, medium-long runs, marathon-pace segments, taper science, recovery spacing. The canonical answer to "what does a serious week look like."
- **Steve Magness** (The Science of Running). Stress-plus-rest adaptation, workout design from physiology up, and a standing critique: individual response variance is large enough that every formula is only a starting point.
- **Luke Humphrey with Keith & Kevin Hanson** (Hansons Marathon Method). The cumulative fatigue doctrine: quality (SOS) days and easy days stacked so the athlete never fully recovers inside the block, by design; the long run capped near 16 miles to simulate the final miles of the marathon on pre-fatigued legs; marathon-pace tempo emphasis; higher frequency over hero long runs.

**The bridge authors.** Fitzgerald and Magness hold citizenship in both wings. That dual presence is the proof the blend is coherent: the field's own best thinkers refused to choose between structure and state. Gierman is a bridge in miniature: a structural rule wrapped in perception doctrine.

## 3. The synthesis thesis

The structure wing defines the **prior**: what training for a goal race should look like before we know anything about today. The state wing defines the **execution layer**: how the athlete reads capacity and runs the session. Scora's verdict is the **arbitration between them**: the canon proposes, physiology and felt state depose, the forecast decides, and verification settles who was right. The canon is where Scora's predictions start; the calibration record is where they stop being anyone else's.

## 4. Adjudication docket (pre-registered conflicts)

1. **Fixed pace prescriptions vs. daily modulation** (Daniels vs. the forecaster). A fitness-derived pace table says today's threshold pace is X; readiness says today it's X plus 15 seconds. Resolution direction: canonical paces are priors with bounds, the verdict modulates within them, and per-athlete verification data decides empirically how much daily modulation actually helps. [INFERENCE, to be tested]
2. **80/20 polarization vs. Dark Side emphasis** (Fitzgerald vs. Diaz). Reconciliation direction: both doctrines agree hard work is scarce and must be protected; they differ on the character and placement of the hard 20%, which is a periodization question, not a contradiction. [INFERENCE, pending primary reading]
3. **Plan integrity vs. the hold verdict** (Pfitzinger vs. restraint doctrine). Key sessions carry the block; a hold that casually deletes them destroys the plan's logic. Resolution direction: the verdict may move or modify a key session but must show its reasoning and log the debt; silent deletion is forbidden. Receipts apply to Scora's interventions too.
4. **Formula vs. individualization** (Magness vs. everyone). His critique lands on the whole canon: response variance breaks every table. Scora's answer is structural: **formulas are day-one priors that decay as personal receipts accumulate.** The calibration record is the individualization engine. This adjudication is close to Scora's entire reason to exist.
5. **Cumulative fatigue vs. the hold verdict** (Hansons vs. restraint doctrine). Hansons blocks design incomplete recovery on purpose; a naive readiness model would call hold every third day and actively fight one of the most proven methods in print. Resolution direction: **verdicts must be block-aware.** Scora forecasts the expected fatigue trajectory of the plan itself; fatigue tracking to plan reads as "hold: proceed as designed," and only deviation from the expected trajectory triggers intervention. "You're tired" is not signal in Hansons week six; "you're tired beyond what this block predicts" is. This deepens restraint from a threshold rule into a model of intent. [INFERENCE, to be built]
6. **Long-run doctrine** (Pfitzinger's 20-22 milers vs. Hansons' 16-mile cap). A genuinely unsettled question in the sport. Resolution direction: treat as an empirical per-athlete question the calibration record can eventually answer; until then, respect whichever doctrine the athlete's chosen plan encodes. [INFERENCE]

## 5. Extraction targets (fill from primary texts)

**Diaz:** operational flow markers; breath as governor or gauge; his heart-rate framework vs. zone culture; Dark Side session architecture and readiness gates; mechanics doctrine; perception training practices; periodization shape; his version of hold; which lab numbers he trusts; direct conflicts with the forecaster thesis.

**Daniels:** how prescriptions adjust for heat, altitude, and fatigue; the stress-points logic; how he handles the gap between race-derived fitness and daily condition.

**Pfitzinger & Douglas:** the readiness signals they respect; spacing rules between quality sessions; what is sacred in a week vs. what flexes; taper mechanics.

**Fitzgerald (80/20):** how moderate is defined and counted; drift detection; whether distribution targets flex by phase. **(By feel):** the practices that train effort perception; how he validates feel against outcomes.

**Magness:** magnitude of individual response variance; which adaptations are slow vs. fast; how he'd instrument appraisal accuracy.

**Hansons:** SOS spacing rules; how cumulative fatigue is expected to feel and progress across the block; their safety valve (when they DO back off, and on what signal); the logic behind the long-run cap as a percentage of weekly volume.

**Gierman / Maffetone:** exact MAF test protocol (warmup, duration, interpretation); what pace regression at capped HR is taken to mean; how the HR cap interacts with felt effort when they disagree.
